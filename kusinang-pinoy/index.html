Their famous dessert

Halo-halo means mix-mix.

On their own menu it’s simply “mix-mix”. It arrives in neat layers. Then you do what every Filipino does: stir, and keep stirring, until the whole glass turns lilac.

The stall

Kusinang Pinoy: the Filipino kitchen.

That’s literally what the name means. A small stall on the Albert Cuyp market, the Filipino flag flying above it, and a sign out front that promises homemade Filipino food & drinks.

Behind the counter is Ate (say “ah-teh”, big sister). She serves rice bowls, lumpia and halo-halo hot and fresh, and reviewers keep mentioning the same thing: how warm the welcome is.

  • Homemade
  • Vegan bowl & veggie soup
  • Tocilog on weekends
